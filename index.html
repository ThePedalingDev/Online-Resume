Approach

The unglamorous parts, done properly.

Most of what I build never makes it into a demo. It's the approval step before a report, the audit log that shows who changed what, and the dashboard someone reads on a bad screen in a site office. That's the part of the job I enjoy, because it's where software earns people's trust.

Resource management
Who has which asset, where, and whether it's compliant.
Audit trails
Append-only records you can query later.
Billing reconciliation
What happened in the field, matched to what was invoiced.
Ops dashboards
React and TypeScript, readable on a bad screen.
Offline-first
Work keeps going without a signal and syncs when it's back.
01

Build for the next team

I write for the version of the system that exists in three years, run by people who aren't me.

02

Get the shape right first

With the right data model, features come cheaply. With the wrong one, every feature costs twice.

03

Make it tell the truth

Audit logs, health checks and dashboards should show what is actually happening, not what you hope is.
